Exercise and physical activity guide

1. Core principles

  • Overload: Fitness improves when the body is gradually challenged beyond its current capacity.
  • Progression: Increase workload slowly over time to support improvement and reduce injury risk.
  • Specificity: Training adaptations match the activity, such as strength work improving force production.
  • Individual differences: Age, health, experience, and goals shape how each person responds.

2. Components of fitness

  • Cardiorespiratory endurance: Aim toward 150–300 minutes of moderate activity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity per week, as appropriate.
  • Strength and endurance: Train major muscle groups with resistance at least two days per week.
  • Flexibility and mobility: Include gentle dynamic or static stretching regularly to support range of motion.
  • Balance: Practice stable, controlled movement to support coordination and everyday function.

3. Training methods

  • Aerobic exercise: Walking, cycling, swimming, and other activities that challenge the heart and lungs.
  • Interval training: Alternating periods of harder effort with lower-intensity recovery.
  • Resistance training: Bodyweight, bands, free weights, or machines used to challenge muscles.
  • Functional training: Exercises that build core stability, coordination, and useful movement patterns.

4. Different needs

  • Children and adolescents: Emphasize enjoyable daily movement, variety, and bone-strengthening activities.
  • Pregnancy: Activity should be adapted to the individual and discussed with a healthcare professional when needed.
  • Older adults: Prioritize balance, mobility, fall prevention, and preserving muscle strength.
  • Chronic conditions: A personalized plan may make low-impact or supervised activity more appropriate.

5. Recovery and safety

  • Warm up and cool down: Prepare gradually and allow your body to return to its resting state.
  • Rest: Sleep and recovery days support tissue repair and help manage training load.
  • Hydration: Replace fluids during activity, especially in heat or during longer sessions.
  • Injury prevention: Use controlled technique, avoid sudden workload increases, and stop for concerning pain.

6. Practical tracking

  • FITT: Consider frequency, intensity, time, and type when planning activity.
  • Track progress: Record sessions, sets, repetitions, distance, or perceived effort to notice gradual changes.
