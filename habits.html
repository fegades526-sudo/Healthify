Healthy habits and wellbeing guide

1. Principles of behavior change

  • Habit stacking: Attach a new habit to an existing routine, such as drinking water after making your morning coffee.
  • Small gains: Focus on manageable improvements instead of changing everything at once.
  • Environment design: Make helpful choices easy to see and unhelpful choices less convenient.
  • Consistency: Small actions repeated regularly usually matter more than occasional intense efforts.

2. Wellbeing pillars

  • Sleep: Keep a consistent schedule, create a comfortable bedroom, and reduce screen use before bed.
  • Daily movement: Add activity throughout the day with walks, stairs, stretching, or standing breaks.
  • Nourishment and hydration: Keep water visible, choose varied foods, and slow down during meals.
  • Morning light: Spend time outdoors early in the day when practical to support a regular sleep-wake rhythm.

3. Cognitive and emotional habits

  • Mindfulness: Set aside a few minutes for breathing or meditation to support focus and emotional regulation.
  • Journaling: Try gratitude lists, a brain dump, or an evening reflection to process thoughts.
  • Digital wellness: Create screen-free spaces, turn off nonessential notifications, and take regular breaks.
  • Stress management: Use slow breathing, brief movement, or a supportive conversation during stressful moments.

4. Social and environmental wellbeing

  • Connection: Nurture relationships with regular check-ins, active listening, and shared time.
  • Boundaries: Say no to nonessential commitments when you need to protect your energy.
  • Nature: Spend time outdoors to refresh your attention and support a sense of wellbeing.
  • Order: Keep living and working spaces manageable to reduce visual distractions.

5. Making habits stick

  • Two-minute start: Make a new habit small enough to begin immediately, such as reading one page.
  • Track progress: Use a simple paper or digital tracker to notice consistency.
  • Plan ahead: Define what you will do, when you will do it, and where it will happen.
  • Recover gently: If you miss a day, return to the habit as soon as you reasonably can.

6. Long-term maintenance

  • Review regularly: Check which habits are helping and adjust the ones that no longer fit.
  • Adapt with life: Change routines around workload, seasons, health, and other responsibilities.
  • Celebrate the process: Notice daily effort and consistency, not only final outcomes.
