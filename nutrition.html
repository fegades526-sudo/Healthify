Nutrition basics

Your Guide to Better Nutrition.

Understanding the fundamentals of nutrition can help you make informed choices about what you eat and how it affects your health.

Include vegetables or fruit, a protein source, and a satisfying source of carbohydrates.

Human nutrition: core principles

1. Healthy nutrition fundamentals

  • Adequacy: Meet your macro- and micronutrient needs without regularly exceeding your energy needs.
  • Balance: Include appropriate proportions of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.
  • Moderation: Limit added sugars, saturated fats, and sodium.
  • Diversity: Eat a wide variety of foods across and within food groups.

2. Macronutrients

  • Carbohydrates: A primary energy source found in whole grains, legumes, fruits, and vegetables. Choose minimally processed sources most often.
  • Proteins: Support growth, repair, and many body processes. Include a mix of plant and animal sources where appropriate.
  • Fats: Support cell membranes and hormone production. Emphasize unsaturated fats from fish, nuts, seeds, and vegetable oils.
  • Water: Supports metabolism, temperature regulation, digestion, and many other essential processes.

3. Micronutrients

  • Vitamins: Vitamins A, B-complex, C, D, E, and K support vision, immunity, neurological function, and metabolism.
  • Minerals: Iron, zinc, calcium, iodine, potassium, and magnesium support blood, bones, nerves, and cardiovascular function.
  • Food first: A diverse diet is usually the best foundation. Supplements should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

4. Different needs at different stages

  • Infants and children: Growing bodies need nutrient-dense foods that provide iron, calcium, protein, and vitamins.
  • Pregnancy and lactation: Requirements for nutrients such as iron, folate, vitamin D, calcium, and protein may increase.
  • Older adults: Pay particular attention to calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B12, protein, and fiber.
  • Special dietary needs: Malnutrition and medical conditions require individualized guidance from a qualified professional.

5. Dietary safety

  • Added sugars: Keep added sugars low and prioritize naturally sweet foods such as fruit.
  • Sodium: Compare labels and choose more fresh foods, especially when managing blood pressure.
  • Fats: Avoid industrial trans fats and replace some saturated fats with unsaturated alternatives.
  • Food safety: Wash hands, prevent cross-contamination, cook foods thoroughly, and store them safely.

6. Practical tools

  • Healthy plate: Build meals around vegetables or fruit, whole grains, and a healthy protein.
  • Meal planning: Prepare simple options in advance and use steaming, baking, or boiling more often than frying.
  • Nutrition tracking: Use reputable food databases when you need detailed nutrient information.
